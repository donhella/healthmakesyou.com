5 simple DIY face masks from your kitchen (and 3 ingredients to keep off your face)

Five gentle homemade face masks made with oats, honey, yogurt, banana and avocado, how to patch test, how often to use them, and three popular kitchen ingredients to keep off your face.

By Jude UchellaFounder, Health makes you
27 Nov 20263 min read

A homemade face mask is one of the easiest bits of self-care there is. Five minutes, a few things from the kitchen, and fifteen minutes to sit still. Searches for face masks are up 50% on Pinterest this month as people look for cosy winter skin rituals.

Kitchen masks won't replace proven skin care. But a few gentle ingredients can leave skin feeling soft, calm and hydrated. Here are five that are safe for most people, and three you'll see online that are best left in the kitchen.

Before you start: patch test

Even food can irritate skin. Dab a little of the mask on your inner arm or behind your ear and wait 24 hours. If there's no redness or itching, go ahead. Skip any ingredient you're allergic to when you eat it.

Always use clean bowls and spoons, make a fresh batch each time, and use it right away.

1. Oat and honey mask (soothing)

  • 2 tablespoons finely ground oats
  • 1 tablespoon honey
  • 1 to 2 tablespoons warm water or milk

Mix to a paste, apply for 10 to 15 minutes, then rinse with lukewarm water. Colloidal oatmeal is a well-known skin soother, and honey helps hold moisture in. A good choice for dry, tight winter skin.

2. Yogurt and honey mask (smoothing)

  • 2 tablespoons plain full-fat yogurt
  • 1 teaspoon honey

Mix and apply for 10 minutes. Yogurt contains lactic acid, a very mild exfoliating acid, so skin may feel smoother afterwards. Plain and unsweetened only.

3. Banana and oat mask (calming)

  • Half a ripe banana, mashed until smooth
  • 1 tablespoon ground oats

Apply for 10 to 15 minutes and rinse. Simple, gentle and good for normal to dry skin.

4. Avocado and yogurt mask (nourishing)

  • A quarter of a ripe avocado, mashed
  • 1 tablespoon plain yogurt

Rich and creamy. Best for very dry skin. If your skin is oily or acne-prone, use a lighter mask instead.

5. Cucumber and aloe mask (cooling)

  • 2 tablespoons blended cucumber, strained
  • 1 tablespoon pure aloe vera gel

Apply for 10 minutes. Cool and refreshing, nice after a day in the cold wind. Keep it in the fridge for a few minutes first for extra cooling.

3 ingredients to keep off your face

1. Lemon juice. It's very acidic and can sting, irritate and dry skin. Citrus juice on skin that then gets sun can cause a burn-like reaction called phytophotodermatitis, which can leave dark patches for weeks.

2. Baking soda. It's very alkaline, much more than your skin, and can disrupt the skin barrier, leaving it dry and irritated.

3. Cinnamon and other strong spices. Cinnamon, ginger and chilli can cause redness, burning and allergic reactions on facial skin.

Toothpaste on spots, vinegar toners and undiluted essential oils fall into the same "please don't" category.

How often to use them

Once or twice a week is plenty. Always follow with a moisturiser, and wear sunscreen during the day.

Who should be careful

If you have eczema, rosacea, active acne or very sensitive skin, keep things simple and check with a dermatologist before trying homemade treatments. Stop and rinse right away if anything stings or burns.

For body skin, our DIY sugar scrub recipes pair nicely with these masks. And for dry winter hands, see our 9 habits that heal cracked hands faster.

The bottom line

The best DIY face masks are the gentlest: oats, honey, plain yogurt, banana, avocado and cucumber. Patch test first, keep it short, and skip the lemon, baking soda and spices. Think of a homemade mask as a calming treat for your skin, and keep relying on a cleanser, moisturiser and sunscreen to do the heavy lifting.
