How to use a gua sha at home, step by step

A simple step-by-step guide to using a gua sha tool on your face, what it can realistically do for puffiness and tension, how to keep it clean, and who should avoid it.

By Jude UchellaFounder, Health makes you
27 Nov 20263 min read

Gua sha is a flat, smooth stone you glide along your face and neck. It comes from traditional Chinese medicine, where a firmer version is used on the body, and it has become one of the most popular self-care rituals on Pinterest. Searches are up 40% this month.

It's simple to do, it feels lovely, and it takes about five minutes. Here's how to use one properly, and what you can honestly expect.

What facial gua sha can do

The research on facial gua sha is limited, so it's best to keep expectations realistic.

  • Ease puffiness for a while. Many people say their face looks less puffy afterwards, especially in the morning. Research hasn't properly tested this claim yet, so think of it as a possible bonus rather than a promise.
  • Ease tension. Many people hold tension in the jaw, temples and forehead. Gua sha works a bit like a massage.
  • Give a temporary glow. The strokes bring blood flow to the surface, so skin can look brighter for a short time.
  • Build a calm habit. A few minutes of slow, quiet attention to yourself has its own benefits.

What it won't do: permanently change your face shape, "sculpt" bone, remove wrinkles, or "detox" your body.

What you need

  • A gua sha tool (rose quartz, jade, stainless steel or other smooth stone)
  • A facial oil or a slippery moisturiser, so the tool glides instead of dragging

Step by step

Always start with a clean face and apply a few drops of oil. Hold the tool almost flat against your skin, at about a 15-degree angle. Use light pressure on the face. Repeat each stroke 3 to 5 times.

  1. Neck. Start at the base of your neck and sweep upwards to your jawline, then do the sides of your neck in downward strokes towards your collarbone.
  2. Jaw. Place the notched edge on your chin and glide along your jawline up towards your ear.
  3. Cheeks. Using the long edge, sweep from the side of your nose out across your cheek to your ear.
  4. Under-eyes. Very gently, with almost no pressure, glide from the inner corner of the eye outwards towards your temple. This skin is delicate.
  5. Brows. Sweep from the middle of your brow outwards and slightly up.
  6. Forehead. Glide from your brows up to your hairline, working across the forehead.

Finish by pressing any leftover oil into your skin.

How often

A few times a week is plenty. Some people do it every morning to help with puffiness. Keep it gentle. Facial gua sha shouldn't hurt, and it shouldn't leave marks. Firmer gua sha on the body can cause red marks called "sha", but that's not the goal on the face.

Keep it clean

Wash your tool with warm water and gentle soap after every use and dry it well. A dirty stone can carry bacteria back to your skin.

Who should skip it or check first

  • Anyone with active acne, open spots, cuts, rashes, sunburn or broken skin on the face
  • People with rosacea or very sensitive skin, which may flare with rubbing
  • People who've just had fillers, Botox or a cosmetic procedure (wait and ask your provider)
  • People who bruise easily, take blood thinners, or have a bleeding disorder

For a bigger effect on puffy eyes, pair gua sha with good sleep and the tips in our guide to whether under-eye patches are worth it. And if you like rituals that calm you down, try our simple morning routine.

The bottom line

Facial gua sha is a gentle massage with a stone. It can ease puffiness and tension for a while and it's a calming ritual, but it won't reshape your face. Use oil, use light pressure, keep the tool clean, and enjoy the five minutes.
