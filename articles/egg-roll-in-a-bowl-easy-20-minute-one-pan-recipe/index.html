How to make egg roll in a bowl in 20 minutes with one pan

A simple one-pan egg roll in a bowl recipe with all the flavour of an egg roll and none of the frying, plus easy swaps for chicken, turkey or tofu and tips to keep the cabbage crisp.

By Jude UchellaFounder, Health makes you
27 Nov 20264 min read

Egg roll in a bowl is exactly what it sounds like: everything you love about the inside of an egg roll, cooked in one pan and served in a bowl, without the wrapper and without the deep fryer. It's one of the most searched dinner recipes on Pinterest's health pages this month, and once you make it, you'll see why.

It takes about 20 minutes, uses cheap ingredients, and works for almost any eating style. If you're cutting back on carbs it fits right in. If you're not, serve it over rice and everyone's happy.

What you need

This makes four servings.

  • 1 pound (450 grams) ground pork, chicken or turkey
  • 1 tablespoon sesame oil (or any cooking oil)
  • 3 cloves garlic, finely chopped
  • 1 thumb-sized piece of fresh ginger, grated
  • 1 small onion, thinly sliced
  • 1 bag (about 14 ounces or 400 grams) coleslaw mix, or half a green cabbage and 2 carrots, shredded
  • 3 tablespoons reduced-sodium soy sauce
  • 1 tablespoon rice vinegar
  • 1 teaspoon sriracha or chilli sauce (optional)
  • 4 spring onions, sliced
  • Sesame seeds to finish

The coleslaw mix is the shortcut that makes this recipe so fast. It's already shredded cabbage and carrot, so there's almost no chopping.

Step-by-step

  1. Brown the meat. Heat the oil in your largest frying pan or a wok over medium-high heat. Add the ground meat and break it up with a spoon. Cook for 6 to 8 minutes until browned all the way through with no pink left. The US Department of Agriculture's safe temperature for ground pork is 160°F (71°C) and for ground chicken or turkey it's 165°F (74°C), if you want to check with a thermometer.
  2. Add the aromatics. Push the meat to one side, add the onion, garlic and ginger, and stir for a minute until fragrant.
  3. Add the cabbage. Tip in the coleslaw mix. It will look like too much, but it shrinks fast. Stir and cook for 3 to 5 minutes.
  4. Season. Add the soy sauce, rice vinegar and sriracha. Toss everything together and taste. Add a splash more vinegar for brightness or a pinch of chilli for heat.
  5. Finish. Turn off the heat, scatter over the spring onions and sesame seeds, and serve straight away.

The biggest mistake is overcooking the cabbage. Take it off the heat while it still has a little crunch. Soft, watery cabbage is where this dish goes wrong.

Easy swaps

  • Chicken or turkey: Lighter than pork and just as good. Add an extra splash of soy sauce because they're milder.
  • Shrimp: Cook the vegetables first, then add raw peeled shrimp for the last 3 minutes until pink.
  • Tofu: Crumble a block of firm tofu into the pan, let it brown well before adding the cabbage, and use a little more soy sauce.
  • More vegetables: Sliced mushrooms, bean sprouts, red pepper or shredded kale all work.
  • Egg on top: A fried egg with a runny yolk makes it more filling and adds about 6 grams of protein.

Why it makes a smart weeknight dinner

This is a meal built mostly from vegetables and protein. Cabbage is a cruciferous vegetable, in the same family as broccoli and Brussels sprouts, and Harvard's Nutrition Source notes that eating a wide variety of vegetables is linked with better heart health. Cabbage is also cheap, keeps for weeks in the fridge, and holds up well to cooking.

Compared with a takeaway egg roll, you skip the deep-fried wrapper, which cuts calories and fat. A homemade bowl made with lean ground turkey comes in at roughly 300 calories and around 25 grams of protein per serving, depending on the meat you choose. Use the USDA's FoodData Central database if you want to work out your own exact numbers.

Keep an eye on the salt

Soy sauce is where most of the sodium in this dish comes from. The US Food and Drug Administration says most Americans eat far more sodium than the recommended limit of less than 2,300 milligrams a day. Using reduced-sodium soy sauce, plus fresh ginger, garlic and vinegar for flavour, keeps it in check without losing taste.

Serving and leftovers

Serve it as it is, over brown rice or cauliflower rice, or wrapped in lettuce cups. Leftovers keep in an airtight container in the fridge for 3 to 4 days. Reheat in a hot pan rather than the microwave so the cabbage doesn't go soggy. It also makes an easy packed lunch, and it fits nicely into our healthy lunch meal prep plan.

The bottom line

Egg roll in a bowl is proof that a healthier dinner doesn't have to be complicated. One pan, a bag of coleslaw and twenty minutes give you a big, savoury bowl of food that tastes like takeaway. If you like this kind of easy swap, try our guide to spaghetti squash as a pasta swap next.
