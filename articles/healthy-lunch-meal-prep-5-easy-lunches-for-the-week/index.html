Healthy lunch meal prep for beginners with 5 lunches that last all week

A beginner's guide to healthy lunch meal prep: one simple formula, five lunches you can make in about an hour, how long each keeps in the fridge, and tips so they still taste good on Friday.

By Jude UchellaFounder, Health makes you
27 Nov 20264 min read

Lunch is the meal that most often goes wrong. You're busy, you're hungry, and the nearest option is a sandwich shop or a drive-through. By Friday you've spent a small fortune on food you didn't really enjoy. No wonder "healthy lunch ideas" and "healthy meal prep" are two of the most searched health topics on Pinterest right now.

Meal prep fixes this, but only if it's simple enough to keep doing. This guide gives you one easy formula, five lunches you can make in about an hour on Sunday, and the food safety basics so your Thursday lunch is as good as your Monday one.

The lunch box formula

Every lunch below follows the same pattern, based on the Harvard Healthy Eating Plate. Fill your container roughly like this:

  • Half vegetables: roasted, raw or salad leaves. The more colours the better.
  • A quarter protein: chicken, eggs, beans, lentils, tofu, fish or lean meat.
  • A quarter whole grains or starchy foods: brown rice, quinoa, whole-wheat pasta, potatoes or whole-grain wraps.
  • A little healthy fat: olive oil dressing, avocado, nuts, seeds or cheese.

Once you know the formula, you can make endless versions from whatever's in your fridge.

5 easy meal prep lunches

1. Chicken, roasted vegetable and quinoa bowls. Roast a tray of chopped peppers, courgette and red onion with olive oil at 425°F (220°C) for 25 minutes. Cook a pot of quinoa and grill or bake some chicken breasts at the same time. Divide between containers and add a lemon and herb dressing on the day.

2. Mason jar salads. Put the dressing in the bottom of a jar, then the sturdy vegetables (carrots, cucumber, peppers), then beans or chickpeas, then grains, then protein, and finally the salad leaves on top. Keep the jar upright and shake it into a bowl at lunchtime. The leaves stay crisp because they never touch the dressing until you're ready.

3. Lentil and vegetable soup. One big pot makes five portions. Soften onion, carrot and celery, add red lentils, a can of chopped tomatoes, stock and spices, and simmer for 25 minutes. Soup freezes well, so you can make a double batch. If you want more ideas, try our 5 healing soup recipes for cold days.

4. Egg roll in a bowl with brown rice. Our one-pan egg roll in a bowl takes 20 minutes and reheats well. Pack it over brown rice for a filling lunch.

5. Turkey and hummus wrap boxes. These are "assemble at lunchtime" boxes. Pack a whole-grain wrap, sliced turkey, a pot of hummus, spinach, grated carrot and cucumber in separate sections. Build the wrap when you eat so it never goes soggy.

How to prep it all in about an hour

  1. Turn the oven on and get the vegetables and chicken roasting first, because they take longest.
  2. While they cook, start the quinoa or rice and the soup on the hob.
  3. Boil a few eggs in another pan if you want them for snacks or salads.
  4. Chop the raw vegetables for the jars and wrap boxes.
  5. Let everything cool before packing, then put it in the fridge.

How long does meal prep keep?

This is the part people skip, and it matters. According to FoodSafety.gov, cooked leftovers such as chicken, soups, grains and cooked vegetables should be eaten within 3 to 4 days when kept in the fridge at 40°F (4°C) or below. That means Sunday's prep covers Monday to Wednesday safely.

The easy fix for a five-day week:

  • Eat the cooked lunches (bowls, soup, egg roll bowls) first, Monday to Wednesday.
  • Freeze soup portions and take one out the night before for Thursday.
  • Make the no-cook wrap boxes and jar salads for Thursday and Friday, or do a quick 15-minute top-up prep on Wednesday evening.

Also cool food quickly. Don't leave cooked food sitting out for more than two hours before it goes into the fridge.

Tips to stop meal prep getting boring

  • Same base, different sauces. One batch of chicken and grains tastes completely different with pesto, a peanut dressing or salsa.
  • Keep wet and dry apart. Pack dressings and sauces in small separate pots.
  • Use good containers. Glass containers with clip lids reheat well and don't hold smells.
  • Add something fresh on the day. A handful of leaves, a squeeze of lemon or some fresh herbs wakes up a three-day-old lunch.

The bottom line

Healthy lunches come down to planning, not willpower. Learn the half-vegetable, quarter-protein, quarter-grain formula, spend an hour on Sunday, and follow the 3-to-4-day rule. You'll eat better, save money, and stop the daily "what's for lunch?" panic. For the other end of the day, our easy 7-day anti-inflammatory dinner plan works on the same idea.
