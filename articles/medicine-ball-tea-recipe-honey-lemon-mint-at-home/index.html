How to make medicine ball tea at home with honey, lemon and mint

How to make the popular coffee-shop medicine ball tea at home with green tea, herbal tea, lemonade and honey, plus a lower-sugar version and what it can and can't do for a sore throat.

By Jude UchellaFounder, Health makes you
27 Nov 20263 min read

Every winter, one coffee-shop drink becomes the unofficial sore-throat order: the "medicine ball". It's a mix of green tea, a peach or herbal tea, steamed lemonade and honey. Searches for medicine ball recipes are up 90% on Pinterest this month.

The good news is that it's very easy to make at home, it's much cheaper, and you can cut the sugar right down. Here's how.

What's in it

The coffee-shop version usually combines:

  • A mint green tea
  • A peach herbal tea
  • Hot water and steamed lemonade
  • Honey

The lemonade is where most of the sugar comes from. A large shop-bought version can contain around 30 grams of sugar or more, much of it from the lemonade and syrup.

Classic homemade medicine ball tea

Makes 1 large mug.

  • 1 mint green tea bag (or plain green tea plus a few fresh mint leaves)
  • 1 peach or other fruity herbal tea bag
  • Three quarters of a cup (180 ml) hot water, just under boiling
  • Half a cup (120 ml) lemonade
  • 1 to 2 teaspoons honey

Steep both tea bags in the hot water for 3 to 4 minutes, then remove them. Warm the lemonade in a small pan or the microwave until steaming, but not boiling. Pour it into the tea and stir in the honey.

Lower-sugar version

This is the one we'd drink regularly.

  • Same two tea bags
  • 1 cup (240 ml) hot water
  • The juice of half a fresh lemon
  • 1 teaspoon honey
  • A slice of fresh ginger (optional)

Steep the teas and ginger for 4 minutes, remove, then add the lemon juice and honey. You keep the bright lemon flavour and the soothing honey, but with a fraction of the sugar.

Tip: green tea tastes bitter if the water is too hot or it steeps too long. Let the kettle sit for a minute after boiling.

Does it actually help?

It won't cure a cold or kill a virus, but it can make you feel better:

  • Warm drinks soothe a sore throat and help loosen congestion. Staying hydrated matters when you're unwell.
  • Honey has reasonable evidence for easing coughs, especially at night, and it coats the throat.
  • Steam from a hot mug can make a blocked nose feel a little clearer.

Watch the caffeine

Green tea has some caffeine, roughly 25 to 35 mg per cup, less than half a cup of coffee. That's fine for most adults, but if you're drinking several mugs a day or having one before bed, swap the green tea for a caffeine-free herbal tea like peppermint or chamomile. The FDA says up to about 400 mg of caffeine a day is generally fine for healthy adults, but less is better if you're pregnant or sensitive to it.

And remember: no honey for babies under 12 months old.

When to see a doctor

Get checked if a sore throat is severe, lasts more than a week, comes with a high fever, a rash, or trouble swallowing or breathing. Strep throat, for example, needs proper treatment.

The bottom line

Medicine ball tea is a warm, soothing drink that's easy to make at home: two teas, lemon and honey. Skip most of the lemonade and you have a comforting, low-sugar mug for sore-throat days.
