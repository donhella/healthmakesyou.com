Habits

How to use a pill organizer so you never miss a dose

How to choose and fill a weekly pill organizer, simple habits to remember your medicines, which medicines shouldn't go in one, and how to stay safe when you take several.

By Jude UchellaFounder, Health makes you
27 Nov 20263 min read

"Did I take my tablet this morning?" If you've ever stood in the kitchen asking yourself that, you're not alone. Forgetting doses, or taking one twice by mistake, is one of the most common medicine problems, especially for people who take several.

A pill organizer is a cheap, simple fix. Searches for them are up 40% on Pinterest this month. Here's how to choose one, fill it properly and make it a habit.

Why it matters

Medicines only work properly when they're taken as prescribed. Missing doses of blood pressure, heart, diabetes or thyroid medicines, for example, can mean they don't control the condition well. Doubling up by accident can cause side effects.

The National Institute on Aging notes that many older adults take several medicines a day, which makes keeping track harder. A clear system takes the guesswork out.

Choosing the right organizer

  • Daily compartments (7 boxes) are fine if you take everything once a day.
  • Morning and evening (AM/PM) organizers suit people who take medicines twice a day.
  • Four times a day versions have morning, noon, evening and bedtime boxes.
  • Monthly or 28-day sets let you fill four weeks at once.
  • Large, easy-open lids help if you have arthritis or weak grip.
  • Clear lids or big print help if your eyesight isn't great.
  • Organizers with alarms or smart apps remind you when it's time, and some alert a family member if a dose is missed.

How to fill it safely

  1. Make a medicine list first. Write down every medicine, the dose, the time of day, and what it's for. Include vitamins, supplements and over-the-counter medicines.
  2. Fill it once a week, at the same time, such as Sunday evening. Do it at a table with good light, without distractions.
  3. Work one medicine at a time. Put one bottle's tablets into every day that needs them, then put that bottle away before opening the next. This stops mix-ups.
  4. Double-check before you close the lids. Count the tablets in each box against your list.
  5. Keep the original bottles. You'll need the labels for doses, expiry dates and refill details.

Medicines that shouldn't go in an organizer

Ask your pharmacist before putting these in a pill box:

  • Medicines that must stay in their original packaging to protect them from light or moisture (some come in foil blister packs for this reason)
  • Medicines you take only "as needed"
  • Tablets that crumble easily
  • Liquids, inhalers, injections and patches, obviously

Many pharmacies can also pre-pack your medicines into dated blister packs for you, which is worth asking about.

Habits that help you remember

  • Link it to something you already do, like brushing your teeth, putting the kettle on, or eating breakfast.
  • Keep the organizer where you'll see it at that time of day, but out of reach of children and pets, and away from heat and steam (not in the bathroom).
  • Set a phone alarm.
  • If you miss a dose, don't automatically double up. Check the leaflet or ask your pharmacist what to do for that particular medicine.

Review your medicines regularly

Bring all your medicines, including supplements, to a pharmacist or doctor at least once a year, or whenever anything changes. Ask whether you still need each one, whether any interact, and whether the timing could be simpler. Fewer daily doses are easier to remember.

The bottom line

A pill organizer, filled carefully once a week and linked to a daily habit, is one of the simplest ways to take medicines correctly. Keep an up-to-date medicine list, ask your pharmacist about anything that shouldn't go in the box, and review your medicines at least once a year.
