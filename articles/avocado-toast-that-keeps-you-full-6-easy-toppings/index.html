How to make avocado toast that actually keeps you full until lunch

How to build avocado toast that keeps you full for hours, with the right bread, a protein boost, six easy topping ideas, and how to stop leftover avocado turning brown.

By Jude UchellaFounder, Health makes you
27 Nov 20263 min read

Avocado toast is one of those breakfasts that sounds healthy, tastes great, and then leaves you raiding the snack drawer at 10:30. That's not the avocado's fault. It's usually the bread, the portion, or the fact that there's almost no protein on the plate.

It's still one of the most searched healthy breakfasts on Pinterest. Here's how to make it properly, so it actually carries you through to lunch.

Why avocado is worth it

Half a medium avocado gives you around 5 grams of fibre, plus potassium and mostly unsaturated fats, the kind linked with better heart health when they replace saturated fats. The fat and fibre slow digestion down, which helps you feel full.

What avocado doesn't have much of is protein. Half an avocado has only about 1 to 2 grams. That's the missing piece in most avocado toast.

Step 1: choose a better bread

Thin white bread digests quickly. Pick something with more fibre and substance:

  • Wholegrain or seeded bread
  • Real sourdough (longer-fermented and often easier on the stomach for some people)
  • Rye bread

If you're not sure your sourdough is the real thing, we made a whole video on how to spot fake sourdough.

Step 2: mash it well

Use half to one small avocado per slice. Mash with a fork and add a pinch of salt, black pepper and a squeeze of lemon or lime. The acid brightens the taste and slows browning. A few chilli flakes are a good idea too.

Step 3: add protein

This is the step that keeps you full. Aim for at least 15 grams of protein in the whole breakfast. Easy ways to get there are in the toppings below.

6 avocado toast toppings that make it a proper meal

1. Eggs. Fried, poached or jammy boiled. Two eggs add about 12 grams of protein. The classic for a reason.

2. Smoked salmon and capers. Add thin slices of smoked salmon, a few capers and some red onion. Rich, savoury and high in protein.

3. Cottage cheese and tomato. Spread a layer of cottage cheese under the avocado, then top with sliced tomatoes and black pepper. A half cup of cottage cheese adds around 12 grams of protein.

4. White beans and lemon. Lightly mash canned white beans with lemon, garlic and olive oil, spread them on the toast and add avocado on top. Good for a plant-based option.

5. Feta and pomegranate. Crumble feta over the avocado and scatter pomegranate seeds and mint on top. Lovely for a winter brunch.

6. Turkey and spinach. Lay a few slices of roast turkey and a handful of baby spinach on the toast before the avocado.

Want more ideas like this? See our list of high-protein breakfasts with about 20 grams each.

How to stop leftover avocado turning brown

Avocado turns brown when the flesh meets air. To save the other half:

  1. Leave the stone in.
  2. Brush the cut surface with lemon or lime juice.
  3. Wrap it tightly in cling film or put it in an airtight container, cut side down.
  4. Keep it in the fridge and use it within a day.

A little browning on top is harmless. Just scrape off the top layer.

Watch the portion

Avocados are healthy, but they're also calorie-dense. A whole large avocado can add more than 300 calories. If you're watching your weight, half an avocado across one or two slices is a good amount.

The bottom line

Good avocado toast is a balance: high-fibre bread, a sensible amount of avocado, and a real source of protein on top. Get those three right and it stops being a snack and becomes a breakfast that lasts.
