Does a hot toddy actually help a cold? The recipe, plus an alcohol-free version

What a hot toddy can and can't do for a cold, a classic recipe, an alcohol-free version that keeps the comfort, and who should skip the whiskey altogether.

By Jude UchellaFounder, Health makes you
27 Nov 20263 min read

When the first cold of the season hits, a lot of people reach for a hot toddy: hot water, honey, lemon and a splash of whiskey. Searches for "hot toddy" on Pinterest are up 80% this month, right on cue.

So does it actually help? Partly. Here's the honest answer, a classic recipe, and an alcohol-free version that keeps most of the comfort.

What helps, and what doesn't

There's no cure for the common cold. It's caused by viruses, and most colds clear up on their own in 7 to 10 days. What a hot toddy can do is make you feel a bit better while you wait.

  • The warm liquid helps you stay hydrated and the steam can make a blocked nose feel looser. Warm drinks are one of the remedies the Mayo Clinic lists as worth trying.
  • Honey has some evidence behind it for calming a cough, especially at night. It also coats a sore throat.
  • Lemon adds flavour and a little vitamin C, though the amount in a slice won't change how long your cold lasts.
  • The whiskey is the weak link. A small amount may make you feel drowsy, but alcohol dehydrates you and tends to make sleep lighter and more broken later in the night. It doesn't fight the virus.

So the helpful parts are the warmth, the fluid and the honey. The alcohol is optional at best.

Classic hot toddy recipe

Makes 1 mug.

  • 1 cup (240 ml) hot water, just off the boil
  • 1 to 2 teaspoons honey
  • 1 tablespoon fresh lemon juice
  • 1 shot (about 30 to 45 ml) whiskey or bourbon
  • Optional: a cinnamon stick, a few cloves pushed into a lemon slice, a slice of fresh ginger

Warm your mug with hot water first, then tip it out. Add the honey, lemon juice and whiskey, top up with the hot water and stir until the honey dissolves. Add the cinnamon, clove-studded lemon or ginger and let it sit for a couple of minutes.

Keep it to one. More alcohol won't make you feel better.

Alcohol-free "cold comfort" toddy

This is the version we'd make when you're actually unwell.

  • 1 cup (240 ml) hot water or hot caffeine-free tea (chamomile or rooibos work well)
  • 2 to 3 thin slices of fresh ginger
  • 1 to 2 teaspoons honey
  • 1 tablespoon fresh lemon juice
  • A cinnamon stick and a pinch of ground turmeric or black pepper, if you like

Steep the ginger and cinnamon in the hot water or tea for 5 minutes. Stir in the honey and lemon and sip while it's warm. It tastes like a toddy, it's hydrating, and it won't spoil your sleep.

If you like ginger, it's good to know its limits too. Our video on side effects of ginger nobody tells you about covers who should go easy.

Who should skip the whiskey

  • Anyone taking cold and flu medicines, sleeping pills, or medicines that cause drowsiness. Many cold remedies already make you sleepy, and some contain paracetamol (acetaminophen), which shouldn't be mixed with much alcohol.
  • Anyone who is pregnant, under the legal drinking age, or avoiding alcohol for any reason.
  • Anyone with a fever who is already struggling to stay hydrated.

And never give honey to a baby under 12 months. It can cause infant botulism, a rare but serious illness.

When a cold needs more than a mug

See a doctor if you have trouble breathing, a high fever that won't come down, symptoms that last more than 10 days, or symptoms that get better and then suddenly get worse. Older adults and people with heart or lung conditions should check in sooner.

For more cold-weather comfort, try our 5 healing soup recipes for cold days.

The bottom line

A hot toddy can be a comforting part of getting over a cold, mostly thanks to the warm water, steam and honey. The whiskey doesn't help much and can hurt your sleep. When you're unwell, the alcohol-free version gives you all of the comfort and none of the downside.
