7 low-carb dinners that still feel like real food

Seven easy low-carb dinner ideas that are filling and family-friendly, plus what "low carb" really means, which carbs are worth keeping, and who should check with a doctor first.

By Jude UchellaFounder, Health makes you
27 Nov 20263 min read

"Low carb" has a reputation for sad plates: a dry chicken breast and a pile of lettuce. It doesn't have to be like that. Some of the most satisfying dinners are naturally low in carbs, because they're built around protein, vegetables and a bit of healthy fat.

"Low carb recipes" and "keto dinner recipes" are both among the most searched health topics on Pinterest this month. Here are seven dinners that cut back on refined carbs without feeling like a punishment.

What does low carb actually mean?

There's no single official definition. A typical diet gets around half its calories from carbohydrates. Many "low-carb" eating plans bring that down to somewhere between 50 and 130 grams of carbs a day. Strict keto diets go much lower, often under 50 grams.

You don't have to follow a strict plan to benefit from these recipes. For most people, simply swapping some of the white bread, white pasta and sugary foods for vegetables and protein is a sensible change on its own.

1. Zucchini noodles with turkey meatballs

Mix ground turkey with an egg, grated parmesan, garlic and Italian herbs. Roll into balls and bake at 200°C (400°F) for about 18 minutes. Spiralize 2 or 3 courgettes (zucchini), warm them in a pan for 2 minutes, and top with the meatballs and a jar of good tomato sauce.

Tip: don't overcook zucchini noodles or they turn watery.

2. Cauliflower fried rice

Pulse a head of cauliflower in a food processor until it looks like rice (or buy it ready-riced). Fry it with garlic, frozen peas and carrots, diced chicken or shrimp and two scrambled eggs. Finish with soy sauce and sesame oil.

3. Stuffed peppers

Halve 4 peppers and remove the seeds. Fill with browned ground beef or turkey mixed with chopped tomatoes, onion, cumin and a little cauliflower rice. Top with cheese and bake at 190°C (375°F) for 25 minutes.

4. Lemon herb salmon with roasted asparagus

Lay salmon fillets and asparagus on one tray. Drizzle with olive oil, lemon, garlic and dill. Roast at 200°C (400°F) for 12 to 15 minutes. That's it, and it looks impressive.

5. Chicken fajita bowls

Slice chicken thighs, peppers and onions, toss with fajita spices and fry in a hot pan. Serve over shredded lettuce or cauliflower rice with salsa, avocado and a spoon of Greek yogurt.

6. Egg roll in a bowl

All the flavour of an egg roll without the wrapper: ground pork or turkey fried with shredded cabbage, carrots, garlic, ginger and soy sauce. We have a full 20-minute egg roll in a bowl recipe if you want the details.

7. Spaghetti squash bolognese

Roast a halved spaghetti squash until the flesh pulls into strands, then top it with your usual meat sauce. Our guide to spaghetti squash as a pasta swap shows how to cook it so it isn't soggy.

Not all carbs are the problem

Cutting carbs works best when you cut the right ones. White bread, sugary drinks, sweets and pastries are easy wins. Beans, lentils, whole fruit, oats and other whole grains are a different story. They come with fibre, which most adults don't get enough of, and fibre helps with digestion, fullness and steadier blood sugar.

A lot of people do well with a "lower carb" middle ground: vegetables and protein at the centre of the plate, plus a small portion of a high-fibre carb.

Who should check first

Talk to your doctor before making big changes to how you eat if you take medication for diabetes or blood pressure, because cutting carbs quickly can change how much medication you need. The same goes if you're pregnant, have kidney disease, or have a history of disordered eating.

Some people feel tired, foggy or get headaches in the first week of a much lower-carb diet. Drinking enough water and not cutting too hard, too fast usually helps.

The bottom line

Low-carb dinners can be generous and tasty. Build them around a good protein, lots of vegetables and some healthy fat, and keep the high-fibre carbs rather than dropping every carb in sight.
