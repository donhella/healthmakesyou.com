What does a high-protein breakfast look like? 7 easy ideas with 20+ grams

Seven quick high-protein breakfast ideas that each give you around 20 grams of protein or more, why protein in the morning helps you stay full, and simple ways to add more without cooking.

By Jude UchellaFounder, Health makes you
27 Nov 20264 min read

A lot of popular breakfasts are mostly carbs. Toast and jam, a bowl of sweet cereal, a muffin with your coffee. They taste good, but many people find they're starving again by ten o'clock. That's one reason "high protein breakfast" and "healthy breakfast ideas" are among the most searched health topics on Pinterest this month.

Adding protein to your first meal is one of the easiest changes you can make. You don't need protein powder or complicated recipes. Here are seven breakfasts that each come in at roughly 20 grams of protein or more, with real food you can find in any supermarket.

Why protein at breakfast helps

Protein takes longer to digest than refined carbohydrates, so a breakfast that includes it tends to keep you satisfied for longer. Your body also needs a steady supply of protein through the day to maintain muscle, which matters more as we get older.

Many people eat most of their protein at dinner and very little in the morning. Spreading it more evenly across your meals is a simple fix. Harvard's Nutrition Source also suggests choosing healthy protein sources such as eggs, dairy, beans, nuts, fish and poultry, and limiting processed meats like bacon and sausages, which are high in salt and linked with health risks when eaten often.

The protein numbers below are rounded estimates using the US Department of Agriculture's FoodData Central database. Brands vary, so use them as a guide.

7 high-protein breakfast ideas

1. Greek yogurt parfait (about 20 to 25 grams). Layer a 200-gram bowl of plain Greek yogurt with berries, a spoon of chopped nuts and a sprinkle of oats. Plain yogurt with your own fruit has far less sugar than flavoured pots.

2. Veggie scrambled eggs on toast (about 20 grams). Scramble three eggs with spinach, peppers or mushrooms and serve on a slice of whole-grain toast. Three large eggs alone give about 18 to 19 grams of protein.

3. Cottage cheese bowl (about 25 grams). One cup of cottage cheese with sliced peaches, cinnamon and a handful of walnuts. If you prefer savoury, top it with cherry tomatoes, cucumber, olive oil and black pepper.

4. Overnight protein oats (about 20 grams). Mix half a cup of oats with half a cup of milk and half a cup of Greek yogurt, plus a spoon of chia seeds. Leave in the fridge overnight. In the morning, top with fruit. It's ready before the kettle boils.

5. Smoked salmon on rye (about 20 grams). Spread a slice of rye bread with cream cheese or cottage cheese, add 60 to 80 grams of smoked salmon, and finish with lemon and dill. Smoked salmon is salty, so this one is best as an occasional treat.

6. Egg muffins (about 20 grams for three). Whisk eggs with chopped vegetables and a little cheese, pour into a muffin tin and bake at 350°F (175°C) for about 20 minutes. Make twelve on Sunday and you have breakfast for the week.

7. Peanut butter banana toast with a glass of milk (about 20 grams). Two tablespoons of peanut butter on whole-grain toast with sliced banana gives around 12 grams. A glass of milk or soy milk adds about 8 more.

Easy ways to add protein to what you already eat

You don't have to change your whole routine. Small add-ons make a big difference.

  • Make porridge with milk instead of water and stir in a spoon of Greek yogurt at the end.
  • Add a boiled egg on the side of your usual toast.
  • Swap a flavoured yogurt for plain Greek yogurt and add your own fruit.
  • Sprinkle hemp seeds, chia seeds or chopped nuts over cereal.
  • Blend a breakfast smoothie with milk and yogurt rather than juice. Our smoothie guide shows how.

What about protein shakes and bars?

They can be handy on a rushed morning, but they aren't magic. Many protein bars are high in added sugar, and shakes don't come with the fibre and other nutrients you get from whole foods. Treat them as a backup, not the plan.

Making it work on busy mornings

The trick is to do the thinking the night before. Overnight oats, egg muffins and boiled eggs can all be made ahead. Keep Greek yogurt, cottage cheese and fruit at the front of the fridge. Pairing a good breakfast with a simple morning routine makes both easier to stick with. Our easy morning routine guide has a 30-minute plan.

The bottom line

A high-protein breakfast doesn't need to be complicated or expensive. Eggs, yogurt, cottage cheese, oats and nut butter will get you to around 20 grams with very little effort. Pick two or three favourites from this list, rotate them through the week, and see how much easier it is to get to lunch without snacking.
