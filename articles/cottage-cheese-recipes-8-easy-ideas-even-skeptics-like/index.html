8 cottage cheese recipes for people who think they hate cottage cheese

Eight easy cottage cheese recipes, from whipped dips to pancakes and pasta sauce, plus why cottage cheese is so high in protein and what to watch on the label.

By Jude UchellaFounder, Health makes you
27 Nov 20263 min read

Cottage cheese has had a full comeback. A few years ago it was the lumpy thing in the back of your grandmother's fridge. Now it's everywhere on Pinterest, whipped into dips, baked into flatbreads and stirred into pasta.

The reason is simple: it's one of the cheapest, easiest sources of protein in the supermarket. And if the texture puts you off, most of these recipes blend it smooth, so you'd never know it was there.

Why cottage cheese is so popular

Half a cup (about 113 g) of regular cottage cheese has roughly 12 to 14 grams of protein, for around 100 calories. It's mostly casein, a slow-digesting milk protein, which is why it keeps you full. It also gives you calcium and some B vitamins.

The thing to watch is salt. That same half cup can carry 350 to 450 mg of sodium, which adds up if you eat it often. Look for "lower sodium" or "no salt added" versions if you're keeping an eye on your blood pressure.

1. Whipped cottage cheese dip

Blend 1 cup cottage cheese with a squeeze of lemon, a small garlic clove, salt, pepper and a drizzle of olive oil until silky. Top with chilli flakes or fresh herbs. Serve with vegetables or crackers. Most people can't tell it apart from a cream cheese dip.

2. Cottage cheese pancakes

Blend 1 cup cottage cheese, 2 eggs, half a cup of oats and a pinch of baking powder. Cook small pancakes in a lightly oiled pan over medium heat. Fluffy, high in protein and great with berries.

3. Creamy tomato pasta sauce

Blend half a cup of cottage cheese into a warm tomato sauce (off the heat) for a creamy "vodka-style" sauce without the cream. Toss with pasta and fresh basil.

4. Savoury breakfast bowl

Top a bowl of cottage cheese with sliced cucumber, cherry tomatoes, olive oil, black pepper and a sprinkle of everything bagel seasoning. Ready in two minutes.

5. Sweet berry bowl

The opposite direction: cottage cheese with berries, a drizzle of honey, cinnamon and some chopped walnuts. It tastes like cheesecake filling.

6. Cottage cheese egg bake

Whisk 6 eggs with 1 cup cottage cheese, spinach, chopped peppers and a little grated cheese. Bake in a dish at 180°C (350°F) for 30 to 35 minutes. It slices well and keeps in the fridge for three to four days, so it doubles as breakfast meal prep.

7. Cottage cheese flatbread

Blend 1 cup cottage cheese with 2 eggs, spread thinly on a lined baking tray and bake at 180°C (350°F) for 30 to 40 minutes until golden and firm. Use it as a wrap or a pizza base. It's a little delicate, so let it cool before lifting.

8. Crispy cottage cheese chips

Small spoonfuls baked until golden and crunchy. We wrote a full guide on how to make cottage cheese chips that actually get crispy.

Tips if you're new to cottage cheese

  • Start blended. If curds bother you, blend it. Recipes 1, 2, 3 and 7 are all smooth.
  • Choose full-fat or 2% for flavour. Fat-free versions can taste flat. The difference in calories is smaller than you'd think.
  • Check the label. Some brands add gums and thickeners. A short ingredient list (milk, cream, salt, cultures) is a good sign.
  • Look for "live and active cultures" if you want the probiotic bacteria some brands contain.

If you like high-protein dairy, you'll find more ideas in our video on what happens when you eat Greek yogurt daily, and in our 15 high-protein snacks.

The bottom line

Cottage cheese is cheap, filling and easy to sneak into meals you already eat. If the texture has always put you off, start with the whipped dip or the pancakes. You may find yourself buying the big tub.
