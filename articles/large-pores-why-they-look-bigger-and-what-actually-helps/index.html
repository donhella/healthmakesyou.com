Skin

Why your pores look bigger, and what actually helps

Why pores look larger with age, oily skin and sun damage, what really makes them look smaller (and what doesn't), plus a simple routine and when to see a dermatologist.

By Jude UchellaFounder, Health makes you
27 Nov 20263 min read

Lean in close to a mirror and your pores can look enormous, especially on your nose and cheeks. Searches for "dilated pores" on Pinterest are up 300% this month, so you're not the only one peering at them.

Here's the honest truth first: you can't shrink pores or close them for good. But you can make them look noticeably smaller, and it doesn't take an expensive routine.

What pores are, and why they look bigger

Pores are the openings of hair follicles. Each one connects to an oil gland. They're supposed to be there: they let oil (sebum) reach the surface to protect your skin.

Pores look larger for a few main reasons:

  • Oily skin. More oil means pores stretch and look more obvious. This is often genetic.
  • Clogging. Oil and dead skin cells collect in the pore. When the plug oxidises, it darkens into a blackhead, which makes the pore stand out.
  • Age. Skin loses collagen and elasticity over the years, so the skin around each pore sags slightly and the opening looks wider.
  • Sun damage. Years of UV exposure break down collagen too, which is why sun-damaged skin often has more visible pores.
  • Squeezing and picking. This stretches the pore and can leave marks.

What actually helps

The American Academy of Dermatology (AAD) suggests a few simple steps to make pores less noticeable.

1. Wash twice a day with a gentle cleanser. Especially if your skin is oily. Use lukewarm water and your fingertips, not a rough scrub.

2. Use products labelled "non-comedogenic" or "won't clog pores". This goes for moisturiser, sunscreen and makeup.

3. Try salicylic acid. It's oil-soluble, so it gets inside the pore and helps clear the build-up. Look for a cleanser or leave-on product with 0.5% to 2% salicylic acid, and start a few times a week.

4. Add a retinoid at night. Retinoids, like over-the-counter adapalene or retinol, help skin renew itself and support collagen over time. Start slowly, two or three nights a week, because they can cause dryness at first.

5. Wear sunscreen every day. Broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher. It protects the collagen that keeps pores looking tight. This is the step most people skip.

6. Remove makeup before bed. Every night.

What doesn't work

  • Cold water "closing" pores. Pores don't open and close like doors. Cold water can briefly make skin look tighter, but it doesn't change pore size.
  • Harsh scrubs. Scrubbing hard irritates skin and can make oiliness worse. The DIY sugar scrub we shared is for the body, never the face.
  • Pore strips every day. They pull out the top of blackheads but can irritate skin, and the pores fill again within days.
  • Squeezing. It stretches pores and risks scars and infection.

A simple routine for large pores

Morning: gentle cleanser, light non-comedogenic moisturiser, sunscreen SPF 30+.

Evening: gentle cleanser (twice if you wore makeup or sunscreen), salicylic acid or retinoid on alternate nights, moisturiser.

Give it at least 6 to 8 weeks. Skin changes slowly.

When to see a dermatologist

If pores, blackheads or acne aren't improving with over-the-counter products, a dermatologist can help. They may suggest prescription retinoids or in-office treatments such as chemical peels or lasers that work on collagen. Also get checked if one "pore" looks different from the rest, is growing, or won't heal.

For more on skin texture, read our guide to what those tiny skin bumps actually mean.

The bottom line

Pore size is mostly down to genes, oil, age and sun. You can't change your pores, but you can keep them clear and protect the skin around them. Gentle cleansing, salicylic acid, a retinoid and daily sunscreen will do more than any trick you see online.
