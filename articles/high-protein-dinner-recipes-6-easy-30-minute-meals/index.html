High-protein dinner recipes you can make in 30 minutes or less

Six easy high-protein dinners that are ready in about 30 minutes, with roughly how much protein each one gives you, simple swaps, and tips for cooking protein safely.

By Jude UchellaFounder, Health makes you
27 Nov 20264 min read

Most people get plenty of protein at dinner by accident. The problem is usually breakfast and lunch. But dinner is still where a lot of us fall back on the same three meals, or on something quick that leaves us hungry again by 9 PM.

Searches for "high protein recipes" on Pinterest are up 300% on last year. So here are six easy dinners that land on the table in about half an hour, each with a rough protein count so you know what you're getting.

How much protein do you actually need?

The basic recommendation for healthy adults is about 0.8 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight per day. For someone who weighs 70 kg (about 155 pounds), that's around 56 grams a day. Many experts suggest a bit more for older adults and for people who are very active, because muscle gets harder to keep as we age.

Spreading it out helps. A dinner with 25 to 35 grams of protein is a solid target for most adults. Every recipe below gets you there.

1. Sheet-pan chicken and vegetables (about 35 g per serving)

Cut 2 chicken breasts into chunks. Toss on a tray with chopped peppers, red onion and broccoli, a little olive oil, salt, pepper, garlic powder and smoked paprika. Roast at 220°C (425°F) for 20 to 25 minutes, until the chicken reaches 74°C (165°F) inside.

Serves 2. Add a scoop of rice or a baked potato if you want it more filling.

2. Garlic shrimp and white bean skillet (about 30 g)

Fry 2 sliced garlic cloves in olive oil for a minute. Add 250 g peeled shrimp and cook for 2 minutes each side. Stir in a drained can of white beans, a handful of spinach, a squeeze of lemon and some chilli flakes. Warm through.

Serves 2. The beans add fibre as well as extra protein, which is a nice bonus.

3. Turkey taco lettuce cups or bowls (about 30 g)

Brown 450 g lean ground turkey with a chopped onion. Add taco seasoning (or cumin, chilli powder, garlic and oregano) and a splash of water. Serve in lettuce cups or over rice with black beans, salsa, a little cheese and plain Greek yogurt in place of sour cream.

Serves 3 to 4. Greek yogurt quietly adds another few grams of protein per spoon.

4. Salmon with lemony lentils (about 35 g)

Season 2 salmon fillets and bake at 200°C (400°F) for 12 to 15 minutes. While it cooks, warm a pouch or can of cooked lentils with chopped tomatoes, a little garlic, lemon juice and parsley.

Serves 2. Salmon also gives you omega-3 fats, which most people don't eat enough of.

5. Egg fried rice with edamame (about 25 g)

Scramble 4 eggs in a hot pan and set aside. Fry 2 cups of cold cooked rice with frozen peas and a cup of shelled edamame. Add soy sauce, a little sesame oil and spring onions, then fold the eggs back in.

Serves 2. This is the best use of leftover rice there is. Use rice that has been cooled quickly and kept in the fridge, not left out on the counter.

6. Tofu and vegetable stir-fry (about 25 g)

Press a block of firm tofu, cut into cubes and fry until golden. Add sliced vegetables (peppers, snap peas, carrots, mushrooms) and a sauce of soy sauce, garlic, ginger, a little honey and lime. Serve with rice or noodles.

Serves 2. A good meat-free dinner that still hits the protein target.

Easy ways to add protein to any dinner

  • Add a can of beans or lentils to soups, stews and pasta sauces.
  • Stir a spoon of Greek yogurt or cottage cheese into sauces once they're off the heat.
  • Use chicken or bone broth in place of water for rice and grains.
  • Top salads and bowls with eggs, chickpeas, tuna or grilled chicken.
  • Keep a bag of frozen edamame or shrimp in the freezer for nights you have nothing planned.

Cook it safely

Use a food thermometer if you have one. Chicken and turkey should reach 74°C (165°F), ground meat 71°C (160°F), and fish 63°C (145°F) or until it flakes easily. Put leftovers in the fridge within two hours and eat them within three to four days.

If you're trying to keep your blood sugar steadier after dinner, the order you eat things can help too. We explain that in our video on eating in the right order to stop blood sugar spikes. And if mornings are your weak spot, try these high-protein breakfast ideas.

The bottom line

You don't need protein shakes or complicated recipes to eat well at dinner. A palm-sized portion of chicken, fish, eggs, tofu or beans, plenty of vegetables and a sensible portion of grains gets most adults to a solid 25 to 35 grams. Pick two or three of these recipes and rotate them through the week.
