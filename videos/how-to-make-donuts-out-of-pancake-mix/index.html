How to Make Donuts Out of Pancake Mix

The full guide

If you have a box of pancake mix in the cupboard, you're most of the way to homemade doughnuts. Pancake mix already contains flour, a raising agent, sugar and salt, so you only need a few extra ingredients to turn it into a soft dough you can shape, fry and glaze. Here's a simple step-by-step recipe, plus tips for getting them right and a few lighter options.

The ingredients you'll need

For about 8 to 10 doughnuts:

  • 2 cups (about 250 g) pancake mix
  • 1 large egg
  • 1/3 cup (80 ml) milk
  • 2 tablespoons melted butter, plus 2 tablespoons sugar (optional, for a sweeter, richer dough)
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • Vegetable or sunflower oil, for frying
  • Extra pancake mix or flour, for dusting

For the glaze:

  • 1 cup (about 120 g) icing sugar
  • 2 to 3 tablespoons milk
  • 1/2 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • Sprinkles, cinnamon sugar or melted chocolate (optional)

Mixing the ingredients

  1. Whisk the egg, milk, melted butter, sugar (if using) and vanilla in a bowl.
  2. Add the pancake mix and stir with a spoon until it comes together.
  3. Tip it onto a floured surface and knead gently for a minute until smooth. It should be soft but not sticky. If it's too sticky, add a little more pancake mix, a spoonful at a time. If it's too dry, add a splash of milk.
  4. Cover and let it rest for 10 minutes. This makes it easier to roll.

The dough is much softer than pancake batter. You want something you can roll and cut, a bit like scone or biscuit dough.

Shaping and frying the doughnuts

  1. Roll the dough out to about 1.5 cm (just over half an inch) thick.
  2. Cut out rounds with a doughnut cutter, or use a large glass for the outside and a bottle cap or piping tip for the hole. Re-roll the scraps once.
  3. Heat about 5 cm (2 inches) of oil in a heavy, deep pan to 175 to 180°C (350°F). Use a thermometer if you have one. Without one, drop in a small piece of dough: it should sizzle and float up within a few seconds.
  4. Fry two or three doughnuts at a time for about 1 to 2 minutes per side, until golden brown.
  5. Lift them out with a slotted spoon and drain on kitchen paper. Fry the doughnut holes too; they cook in under a minute.

Glazing and decorating

  1. Whisk the icing sugar, milk and vanilla into a smooth glaze. It should coat the back of a spoon.
  2. Dip the top of each warm (not hot) doughnut into the glaze and lift, letting the excess drip off.
  3. Add sprinkles straight away, before the glaze sets.

For cinnamon sugar doughnuts, skip the glaze and toss the warm doughnuts in a mix of sugar and a little ground cinnamon. For chocolate, dip them in melted chocolate instead.

Tips and tricks for perfect pancake mix doughnuts

  • Control the oil temperature. Too hot and the outside burns before the middle cooks; too cool and the doughnuts soak up oil and turn greasy.
  • Don't overcrowd the pan. Too many at once drops the temperature.
  • Don't overwork the dough. Knead just enough to bring it together, or the doughnuts will be tough.
  • Check one first. Cut your first doughnut open to make sure it's cooked through, and adjust timing.
  • Let them cool slightly before glazing, or the glaze will melt and slide off.

Kitchen safety

Hot oil is dangerous. Never leave the pan unattended, keep children and pets away, and don't fill the pan more than a third full. Lower the doughnuts in gently with a spoon to avoid splashes. If the oil starts to smoke, turn off the heat. Never put water on an oil fire; cover the pan with a lid or a damp tea towel and turn off the heat. Let the oil cool completely before disposing of it, and never pour it down the sink.

Lighter options

Doughnuts are a treat, and they're best enjoyed that way: occasionally, and in reasonable portions. If you'd like a lighter version:

  • Bake them in a doughnut tin at about 180°C for 10 to 12 minutes, using a slightly wetter batter.
  • Try an air fryer: spray lightly with oil and cook at about 180°C for 5 to 6 minutes, turning halfway.
  • Make them smaller or just make doughnut holes, so a portion is a few bites.
  • Go easy on the glaze, or use a light dusting of cinnamon sugar instead.

The bottom line

Pancake mix is a quick shortcut to homemade doughnuts: add egg, milk, butter and vanilla, knead into a soft dough, cut, fry at the right temperature and glaze. Keep a close eye on the oil, don't overcrowd the pan, and enjoy them as an occasional homemade treat.
