The Benefits of Sheep Milk You Never Knew About

The full guide

Sheep milk is the milk behind some of the world's best-loved cheeses, including feta, pecorino, Roquefort and manchego, but few people ever drink it by the glass. That's a shame, because it's one of the most nutrient-dense milks you can buy. Here's what makes sheep milk different, the benefits that come with it, how it compares with cow and goat milk, and who should go easy on it.

The nutritional profile of sheep milk

Sheep milk is much richer than cow or goat milk. According to US Department of Agriculture data, a cup (about 245 ml) gives you roughly:

  • About 15 grams of protein, close to double the 8 grams in a cup of cow milk.
  • About 470 mg of calcium, around 50% more than cow milk.
  • More fat and calories, at roughly 265 calories a cup, because sheep milk is naturally creamy.
  • Good amounts of riboflavin, vitamin B12, phosphorus, magnesium and zinc.

The high level of solids is exactly why sheep milk makes such rich cheese and thick yoghurt: you need less milk to make the same amount.

Benefit 1: more protein in every glass

Protein keeps you full, helps your muscles repair after activity, and becomes more important as we age, when the body naturally starts to lose muscle. Because sheep milk is so protein-rich, a small glass goes a long way.

That makes it a useful option for people who struggle to eat enough, such as older adults with a smaller appetite or anyone recovering from illness, and for active people who want protein from whole foods rather than powders.

Benefit 2: strong support for bones and teeth

Bones need calcium, but they also need phosphorus, magnesium, zinc and protein, and sheep milk supplies all of them together. With around half as much calcium again as cow milk in each cup, it helps you reach your daily target with less.

As with any milk, it works best alongside movement. Walking, climbing stairs and lifting things are what signal your bones to stay strong; the milk provides the building materials.

Benefit 3: B vitamins for energy

Sheep milk is a good source of riboflavin (vitamin B2) and vitamin B12. Riboflavin helps your cells turn food into energy, and B12 is needed to make red blood cells and keep nerves healthy. Low B12 can leave you tired, foggy and weak, so foods that provide it are especially useful if you eat little meat.

Benefit 4: easier on some stomachs

Like goat milk, sheep milk has smaller fat globules than cow milk and a high share of medium-chain fatty acids, which the body digests and uses for energy quickly. Some people find it sits more comfortably than cow milk.

Here's the honest part about lactose, though. Sheep milk contains about the same amount of lactose as cow milk, sometimes a little more. So it isn't a solution for people with true lactose intolerance. Where sheep milk does help is in fermented and aged forms: sheep milk yoghurt has some of its lactose broken down, and hard sheep cheeses such as pecorino and manchego contain very little. Many people who are lactose intolerant handle these well in normal portions.

How it compares with other milks

  • Versus cow milk: sheep milk has nearly twice the protein, much more calcium and more fat and calories. Cow milk is lighter and cheaper, and often fortified with vitamin D.
  • Versus goat milk: both have small fat globules and a creamy texture, but sheep milk is richer still, with more protein, fat and calcium. Our goat milk vs cow milk video covers that comparison in more detail.
  • Versus plant milks: most plant milks are far lower in protein and only contain calcium and B12 if they've been fortified.

Who should be careful?

  • People with a cow's milk allergy. Sheep milk proteins are very similar to cow milk proteins, so it isn't a safe alternative for a milk allergy.
  • People watching their calories. Sheep milk is rich, so a small glass is plenty. Think of it as a nourishing food, not a drink to gulp down.
  • Babies under 12 months. No animal milk should be a baby's main drink before their first birthday.
  • Anyone tempted by raw milk. Choose pasteurised sheep milk and cheeses. Raw milk can carry harmful bacteria and is especially risky for pregnant women, young children and older adults.

How to enjoy it

Sheep milk is easiest to find as yoghurt and cheese, and that's a great place to start: Greek-style sheep yoghurt with fruit, a little feta crumbled over a salad, or pecorino grated over vegetables. If you can find the milk itself, try a small glass chilled, or use it in porridge, smoothies or baking for extra creaminess.

The bottom line

Sheep milk is one of the richest milks you can drink, with almost double the protein of cow milk, plenty of calcium and useful amounts of B vitamins, phosphorus and zinc. Many people find it creamy and comfortable to digest, though it isn't low in lactose and isn't safe with a milk allergy. Enjoy it in small portions, especially as yoghurt and aged cheese, and it can be a very nourishing part of your diet.
