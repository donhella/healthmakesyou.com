Best foods for seniors - 7 nutritious choices after 60

The full guide

The best foods for seniors aren't complicated, expensive or hard to find. Many of the foods that help protect your strength, heart, brain, bones, digestion and independence after 60 are ones you already know. Healthy ageing is less about discovering something new and more about eating the right foods consistently. Here are seven nutritious choices that deserve a regular place on your plate, why they matter more as you get older, and the one that needs a little care with certain medicines.

1. Salmon

Salmon gives you several things your body needs with age in one food. It provides high-quality protein, which helps you keep muscle, and the omega-3 fats EPA and DHA, which support heart health and play important roles in the brain. It's also one of the few foods that naturally contains vitamin D, which works with calcium to support your bones.

After 60, protecting muscle isn't about looking fit. It's about being able to stand up, walk comfortably, climb stairs, carry things and stay independent. Bake, grill or air-fry salmon, or add it to a salad with vegetables and whole grains. Frozen and tinned salmon work well too and cost less; just check the salt on tinned varieties if you're watching your blood pressure.

2. Sweet potatoes

Sweet potatoes give you much more than carbohydrate. They provide fibre, potassium, vitamin C and lots of beta-carotene, which your body turns into vitamin A for vision and immunity. Potassium helps with muscle function, fluid balance and blood pressure, and the fibre aids digestion and makes meals more filling.

They're easy to build a meal around: boil, roast or bake them and pair with eggs, fish, chicken, beans or vegetables. They're also soft, which helps if harder foods are difficult to chew. They're still a carbohydrate, though, so if you're managing your blood sugar, keep the portion sensible and pair them with protein and vegetables.

3. Avocado

Avocado is useful for its healthy fats. It's mostly unsaturated fat, along with fibre, potassium and folate, and it fits well into a heart-friendly diet, especially when it replaces foods high in saturated fat. "Replaces" is the key word: avocado instead of butter or creamy spreads moves a meal in a better direction, while piling it on top of an already heavy meal doesn't.

It's soft, easy to eat and versatile: on wholegrain toast, with eggs, in salads or alongside fish. Its fat and fibre make meals more satisfying. It is calorie-dense, though, so half an avocado is usually plenty.

4. Berries

Blueberries, strawberries, raspberries and blackberries give you fibre, vitamin C and plant compounds called polyphenols, including the anthocyanins that give them their deep colours. These compounds have been studied for possible benefits for the heart and brain. A bowl of blueberries won't guarantee a perfect memory at 90, but berries are nutrient-rich whole fruits that fit easily into a healthy ageing diet.

Add them to Greek yoghurt or porridge, blend them into a smoothie or eat them on their own. Frozen berries without added sugar are just as good and often cheaper. They're also a great swap when you want something sweet after a meal instead of cake or biscuits.

5. Broccoli

Broccoli gives you fibre, vitamin C, vitamin K, folate and useful plant compounds. Vitamin C supports immunity and collagen production, vitamin K is needed for normal blood clotting and bone health, and the fibre supports digestion and regularity, which matters more with age, especially if you move less or take medicines that affect your bowels.

It's low in calories but high in nutrients, which is useful because calorie needs often fall with age while the need for vitamins, minerals and protein doesn't. If you don't like it raw, steam or roast it with a little olive oil and seasoning, or add it to soups and egg dishes.

The medication note: if you take warfarin or another medicine affected by vitamin K, keep your intake of vitamin K-rich vegetables like broccoli steady, rather than making big sudden changes, and follow the advice your doctor or anticoagulation clinic has given you.

6. Cottage cheese

Cottage cheese is a surprising pick for staying strong. It gives you a lot of protein in a soft food that needs no preparation. Many older adults find their appetite shrinks, and a big plate of meat isn't always appealing, so cottage cheese offers an easier way to get protein in. Eat it with fruit, vegetables, wholegrain crackers or toast, at breakfast or as a snack.

It also provides calcium, phosphorus and vitamin B12, and its casein protein is digested slowly, releasing amino acids over a longer time. On its own it won't stop age-related muscle loss; you still need enough total protein and some strength exercise. Check the label, because some brands are surprisingly high in salt.

7. Olive oil

Olive oil's benefit comes from what it replaces. It's rich in monounsaturated fat and is the main fat in Mediterranean-style eating, which is strongly linked with heart health. Drizzle a little over vegetables, use it in salad dressings, cook with it, or add it to beans and fish.

It's still very calorie-dense, so the goal isn't to pour it over everything. Swapping some butter or other solid fats for olive oil improves the overall fat balance of your diet. Extra-virgin olive oil also contains polyphenols. Like every food here, it works best as part of a bigger picture of vegetables, beans, fish and whole grains.

The bigger lesson

Nutrition after 60 is about protecting what you rely on every day: your strength, mobility, heart, brain, digestion and bones. Salmon gives you protein and omega-3s, sweet potatoes give fibre and useful carbohydrate, avocado and olive oil provide unsaturated fats, berries and broccoli add fibre and plant compounds, and cottage cheese makes protein easier. No single food guarantees a longer life, but together they support the kind of diet that helps you enjoy your later years.
