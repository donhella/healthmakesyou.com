Apple peel benefits - 7 reasons you should stop peeling your apples

The full guide

If you peel your apples before eating them, you're not alone: plenty of people do it out of habit, or because a parent always did. But the skin is one of the most valuable parts of the fruit. Here are seven reasons to stop peeling your apples, and how to wash them properly so you can eat the skin with confidence.

1. You lose a big share of the fibre

A medium apple with the skin on gives you around 4 to 4.5 grams of fibre. Peel it and that drops noticeably, because much of the fibre sits in the skin and the layer just underneath. Fibre keeps your digestion moving, helps you feel full and supports steady blood sugar, and most adults don't get enough of it. The NHS recommends 30 grams a day, and the average adult eats far less.

Leaving the peel on is one of the easiest fibre upgrades you can make, with no extra cost or effort.

2. Plant compounds hiding in the skin

Apple skin is packed with natural plant compounds, including quercetin, catechins and triterpenoids, at much higher levels than in the flesh. Researchers at Cornell University reported in 2003 that apple peels had far higher antioxidant activity than the flesh, and that peels contained most of the apple's quercetin. These compounds are part of what makes apples so good for you, and peeling throws them in the bin.

3. It feeds your gut

The peel contains insoluble fibre, which adds bulk and helps keep you regular, along with some of the apple's pectin and polyphenols. Pectin and polyphenols act as food for the friendly bacteria in your gut, which turn them into short-chain fatty acids that help keep the gut lining healthy. Keeping the peel on gives those bacteria more to work with.

4. Support for your heart

Whole apples have been linked to better heart health. In one trial, adults with mildly raised cholesterol who ate two whole apples a day for eight weeks saw their total and LDL cholesterol come down. The fibre and polyphenols that help produce effects like this are concentrated in and just under the skin, so eating the whole apple, peel included, is the way to get the full benefit.

5. More antioxidants per bite

Antioxidants help protect your cells from damage caused by unstable molecules called free radicals, which are linked to ageing and chronic disease. Because apple skin contains so many of these compounds, an unpeeled apple delivers more antioxidant power than a peeled one. Red-skinned varieties also contain anthocyanins, the pigments that give them their colour.

6. A more satisfying snack

An unpeeled apple takes longer to chew and contains more fibre, which helps it fill you up. Research has shown that eating whole apple slices before a meal can reduce how much people eat at that meal, while apple sauce or juice has much less effect. The skin adds to that chewy, filling texture, so you're more likely to feel satisfied until your next meal.

7. The simplest reason of all

Peeling takes time, creates waste and leaves you with less fruit. Eating the apple whole is quicker, cheaper and less wasteful. It also makes apples a truly grab-and-go snack: wash it, and it's ready.

How to wash apples properly

Many people peel apples because they're worried about dirt, wax or pesticide residues. Washing well deals with most of this.

  • Rinse the apple under cool running water for about 20 to 30 seconds.
  • Rub the skin firmly with your hands, or use a clean vegetable brush.
  • Dry it with a clean cloth or paper towel, which removes more residue.
  • There's no need for soap or special produce washes. The US Food and Drug Administration advises plain running water.

The wax on shop-bought apples is a thin food-grade coating and is safe to eat. If you'd still rather avoid it, a quick scrub under warm water helps.

When peeling might make sense

There are a few exceptions. Very young children and people with swallowing difficulties may find peeled apple easier and safer to eat. Some people with sensitive digestion, or during a flare of a bowel condition, may tolerate peeled fruit better for a while. And in recipes like smooth purées or some baking, peeling is part of the method. For everyday snacking, though, keep the skin on.

The bottom line

The apple peel is where much of the fibre, the plant compounds and the antioxidants live. Eating it supports your gut, your heart and your appetite, and it saves you time and waste. Wash apples well under running water, rub them dry, and eat them whole. For more on what the whole fruit does for you, see Apple benefits: 9 things you do not know.
